Quotidian Privacy Policy

Effective October 22, 2026

Quotidian is an app for sharing your daily game scores with friends. This page explains what the app collects, why, who can see it, and how to delete it.

What we collect

When you sign in. You sign in with Apple or Google. We receive your name, your email address (or Apple's private relay address, if you chose to hide yours), and an account ID from that provider. We never see your Apple or Google password.

Your profile. Your display name, the @handle you choose, and your time zone.

Your avatar. The small customizable character you pick (a few choices such as color, eyes and mouth). It is stored on your profile and your friends can see it.

Your scores. When you share a game result to Quotidian, we store the game, the text you shared (for example a Wordle result), the score we read from it, the optional short message you add (up to 67 characters), and the time you posted it.

Your streaks. Counts of consecutive days you posted a game, worked out from your posted scores.

Your friends. Who you are friends with, and friend requests you have sent or received.

Krillion answers (coming soon). If you choose to add your Krillion answers from a screenshot, we store the answers text we read from it (the prompts, your answers, and points), not the screenshot itself. The image is read on your phone and discarded. Friends who have already played that day's Krillion can see your answers. People who haven't played yet cannot.

Safety records. People you block, and reports you send about other people.

Sign in with Apple token. If you sign in with Apple, we keep a token Apple gives us. We use it for one thing: telling Apple to cut the connection when you delete your account.

On your device. The app keeps a saved copy of your recent scores and friends so it can open without a connection, and a queue of scores waiting to send. These stay on your phone and are deleted when you sign out or delete your account.

We do not collect your contacts, location, photos, or advertising identifier. We do not use analytics or advertising tools, and we do not track you across other apps or websites.

Who can see it

  • Your friends can see your display name, your handle and your avatar. Friends you have accepted can also see your scores and messages from today and past days, your best score for each game (an all-time best card) with the date it was set, and your stats, such as streaks, games played and averages. Only accepted friends can see this. Nothing is public. If you unfriend or block someone, they lose access.
  • No one else can see your profile or scores. You add friends by @handle. Anyone who knows or guesses your handle can send you a request, but a request does not show them your scores. You choose whether to accept.
  • Service providers. Your data is stored with Supabase, a hosted database and sign-in provider that runs the Quotidian backend. Apple and Google handle the sign-in step. We do not sell your data or share it for advertising.
  • Legal and safety. We may disclose information if the law requires it, or to protect people from harm.

How long we keep it

We keep your data until you delete your account. Your score history is kept until you delete your account, which deletes your history, best scores, stats and Krillion answers. Reports you send, or that others send about you, may be kept after an account is deleted so we can handle abuse.

Deleting your account and your data

In the app, open your profile and tap Delete account. This permanently deletes your profile, scores, friendships and blocks, and it tells Apple to revoke the Sign in with Apple connection. Your scores disappear from your friends' feeds. This cannot be undone.

You can also email us at the address below and we will delete it for you.

Your choices

You can change your display name and handle in the app, remove friends, and block people at any time. You can ask us what we have stored about you by emailing us.

Children

Quotidian is not for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and we will delete it.

Changes

If we change this policy in a meaningful way, we will update the date above and, where it matters, tell you in the app.
